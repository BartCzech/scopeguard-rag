# Competitive Intel: RivalShield AI

**Last Updated:** October 2024
**Owner:** Product Marketing

RivalShield AI is currently our primary competitor in the LLM access control space. They come up in roughly 40% of our enterprise sales deals. Below is a breakdown of their product strategy, pricing, and how we beat them.

### Product & Technical Approach
* **Their Approach:** Post-retrieval filtering and Prompt Injection firewalls. They sit *between* the vector database and the LLM, acting as a proxy. They analyze the retrieved context and use a smaller, faster LLM to redact sensitive information before passing it to the main generation model.
* **Our Advantage:** Our pre-retrieval approach is structurally more secure. RivalShield's method adds significant latency (often +800ms) because they have to process the data twice. Furthermore, they suffer from context fragmentation—if they redact 50% of the retrieved chunks, the final LLM doesn't have enough context to give a good answer. We just don't retrieve the bad chunks in the first place.

### Pricing Intel
*(Gathered from recent win/loss debriefs)*
* They are undercutting us on price for smaller teams. They typically charge $10/user/month for their base tier (compared to our $15).
* However, for Enterprise deals, they charge massive setup fees (often upwards of $50K) for on-premise deployments. 

### Win/Loss Patterns
**Why we win:**
1.  **Latency:** Engineering teams hate the delay RivalShield adds to the pipeline.
2.  **Security Audits:** CISOs prefer our mathematical zero-trust approach over RivalShield's probabilistic redaction model.

**Why we lose:**
1.  **Ease of Setup:** RivalShield requires zero changes to the vector database infrastructure. It's a pure API proxy. Lazy teams sometimes prefer this over setting up proper scope metadata on their embeddings. 
2.  **Feature Breadth:** They recently added a DLP (Data Loss Prevention) feature for outbound prompts, which we currently do not support. 

**Battlecard Summary:**
When selling against RivalShield, hammer on latency and the fundamental insecurity of post-retrieval filtering. "You can't redact a secret fast enough once it's out of the database."
