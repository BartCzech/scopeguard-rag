**By Bart Check, Co-founder & CEO**

The phrase "zero trust" has dominated cybersecurity for the past decade, usually in the context of network access. The core tenet is simple: never trust, always verify. But as organizations rapidly adopt Generative AI and build internal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines, they are inadvertently leaving zero-trust principles at the door. 

When an employee prompts an internal AI agent, the typical system assumes that because the user is authenticated into the application, the LLM should have blanket access to the underlying vector database to construct its answer. This is a massive vulnerability. It relies on perimeter security for an architecture that inherently requires data-level security. 

Building a zero-trust RAG pipeline means applying verification to the retrieval step itself. 

At ScopeGuard, we believe that identity and access management (IAM) must travel with the query. A zero-trust RAG architecture requires three things:
1. **Identity Context:** The system must know exactly who is asking the question and what their roles and permissions are at that exact millisecond.
2. **Metadata Tagging:** Every chunk of data in your vector store must be cryptographically or logically tagged with its required access scope.
3. **Middleware Enforcement:** An immutable layer between the LLM and the database that enforces the intersection of the user's identity and the data's scope.

You cannot achieve zero trust by asking the language model to behave. You achieve it by ensuring the model is physically incapable of retrieving data it has no business seeing.
