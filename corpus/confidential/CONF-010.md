# PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL: ATTORNEY-CLIENT COMMUNICATION

**TO:** Bart Check (CEO), HR Department
**FROM:** Fenwick & West LLP (Employment Practice Group)
**DATE:** November 12, 2024
**SUBJECT:** Feasibility of Modifying Vesting Schedules for Underperforming Employee

### Background
Management has inquired whether it is legally permissible to halt or modify the stock option vesting schedule for an early engineering employee (Employee X) who was granted 0.8% equity upon joining in 2023. Employee X is currently underperforming and is being placed on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP), but is scheduled to hit their one-year vesting cliff next month. 

### Legal Analysis
Under California labor law and the terms of ScopeGuard’s standard 2023 Equity Incentive Plan, unvested options are contingent upon continued service, but the vesting schedule itself constitutes a binding contract. 

**1. Unilateral Modification:** ScopeGuard cannot unilaterally freeze or modify the vesting schedule while the employee remains actively employed. Attempting to do so would likely constitute a breach of contract and could trigger a constructive discharge claim.

**2. Clawbacks:** Our standard option agreements do not contain performance-based clawback provisions for vested shares, only provisions related to termination for "Cause" (e.g., fraud, gross misconduct). Underperformance on a PIP does not meet the legal threshold for "Cause."

### Recommendation
We strongly advise against attempting to alter the vesting schedule. If the company wishes to prevent Employee X from reaching the cliff, the legally sound path is to accelerate the performance management process. If termination is warranted based on the PIP, it must occur prior to the cliff date. 

Alternatively, if the company terminates Employee X without Cause before the cliff, standard practice would involve offering a severance package in exchange for a comprehensive release of claims.
